# `asr probe`: settle the open ASR questions before the full run

**Parent:** <https:../2026-11-24-000-vo-transcripts.md> (wave 3)

## Problem Statement

Unit 005 will transcribe Yamato's Japanese VO clips in the background,
following the proposed design in
`docs/dev/decisions.kb/ml-observations-are-committed-ledgers.md`. A POC on
16 clips with large-v3-turbo has already run (the plan's bullets, and
`trash/vo-transcripts/poc/`). Before 005 is briefed, measure what that
design still guesses at, so the brief carries numbers instead of guesses.

Use the POC's 16 clips as the fixed test set (its `out.turbo/*.txt` is the
baseline), plus any clips a question needs. The clips are in
`trash/vo-transcripts/002/bulk/sounds/vo/yamato/` and the turbo model is in
`trash/vo-transcripts/poc/models/`. Everything here is investigation:
scratch and models go in `trash/vo-transcripts/004/`. Don't build pipeline
code.

**Amended (coordinator, after unit 003 landed):** `data/vo/lines.tsv`
(column `english`) shows that Yamato's English-spoken clips already carry
correct English captions, with the proper nouns right ("Lash's down
there!", "Urn is here!"). Her caption is exactly "Speaks Japanese" on 532
clips (521 top-level, 11 `ping/`), and 57 clips have no caption at all.
So ASR is needed only for the "Speaks Japanese" clips, the language is
known in advance (`-l ja`, no detection pass), and English proper nouns
are not a problem. The old questions 3 and 4 are replaced below. If you
have already measured parts of them, keep the numbers in the doc, but
spend no more time on them.

**Amended again (owner ruling, 2026-11-24):** the bar is *intelligible*
output, not accurate output (see the plan's Problem Statement). A fix-up
pass turns the transcript into good English afterwards. Every clip is
pinned to `-l ja`, including the probably 5 to 10% that are really
English: check only that those still come out intelligible (katakana
English is fine). There is no prompting and no language-label check. Pick
the fastest setup that is still intelligible.

Questions, in order of how much they could change the design:

1. **Batching.** Can several clips share one encoder window? Concatenate
   about 5 to 10 "Speaks Japanese" clips with about 1 s of silence between
   them (ffmpeg), transcribe with `-l ja`, and split the output back by
   segment timestamps. What matters is that each segment maps to the right
   clip and stays intelligible; small wording drift from the solo run
   doesn't. Report the speedup per clip.
2. **Model.** Find the fastest model whose Japanese stays intelligible on
   the test set's Japanese clips. Candidates: turbo f16 (baseline), turbo
   `q5_0`, `medium` `q5_0`, and `small`, from
   `huggingface.co/ggerganov/whisper.cpp`. Check disk first (Disk rule).
   Also run 3 English-spoken clips through it with `-l ja` and confirm the
   output is still intelligible. Recommend one.
3. **Uncaptioned clips.** What are the 57 clips with no caption (`emote/`
   grunts?), and should ASR skip them?
4. **Throughput layout.** Compare 1 process at 4 threads, 2 at 2 threads,
   and 3 at 4 threads. Report clips per hour and how responsive the machine
   stays (load, and whether a foreground `pytest` slows down). The owner
   wants to keep working in the foreground during the run.
5. **CRC stability.** `data/manifest.jsonl` is from an older build and holds
   `crc32` for every `vsnd_c`. Compare it against the current pak index for
   Yamato's VO. How many unchanged paths kept their CRC? That tells us
   whether CRC keying will rerun only the clips that really changed.

**Amended a third time (2026-11-24): low utilization from now on.** Follow
the CPU rule in this kb's `CLAUDE.md`: everything under `nice -n 19`, at
most 4 threads total. For question 4, compare only layouts within that
budget (1×4, 2×2, 4×1), and drop the 3×4 layout. For batching, the
coordinator saw in your `batch/*.json` that every model except turbo f16
merges neighbouring clips into one segment. Splitting by segment therefore
fails, so test assigning word-level timestamps (`-ml 1` or `-sow`) to the
known clip offsets in the concatenation, and a longer silence gap. The
model pick is the fastest one whose output stays intelligible and splits
reliably that way.

Write the durable findings (tool behavior, speeds, flags on this host) into
a new `docs/dev/environment.kb/whisper-asr.md`, and add the tooling (brew
`whisper.cpp` 1.9.5, `ffmpeg` 9.1) to `environment.kb/toolchain.md`. Archive
any script the doc cites in `docs/dev/probes/asr-*`. Put the
recommendations for 005 and for the design doc in your Final report. Don't
edit the design doc: the coordinator owns it.

**Scope (only these files):** `docs/dev/environment.kb/whisper-asr.md`
(new), `docs/dev/environment.kb/toolchain.md`, `docs/dev/probes/asr-*`,
`docs/dev/formats.kb/asset-extensions.md` (fix `vsnd_c -> wav`: VRF writes
the stored MP3), this file. Scratch: `trash/vo-transcripts/004/`.
